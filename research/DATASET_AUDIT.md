# Dataset Audit: Daylight-Aware Indoor Inverse Rendering

**Purpose.** Survey existing real-world and synthetic datasets for suitability in extending IRIS (CVPR 2025) to explicitly model outdoor daylight entering indoor scenes through windows — direct sunlight, sun patches on floors/walls, sky illumination, window transmission, and changing solar direction. Evaluated against 12 target properties: indoor scene, strong daylight, visible windows, visible cast sun patches, multi-view imagery, calibrated poses, usable geometry, multiple illumination conditions/times of day, capture timestamp, geo-location/orientation, HDR/RAW or calibrated exposure, and academic-publication-safe licensing.

Research method: web search of dataset project pages, papers (arXiv/CVF/ACM/ScienceDirect), GitHub repos, and IRIS/FIPT's own project pages and teaser figures (`assets/teaser_cvpr25.png` inspected directly). No claims below are guessed from model memory alone without a citation trail; where evidence was not found, the cell is marked `unknown`.

## Scoring rubric (0–10, applied to genuine fitness for the FULL task, not any single property)

- **8–10 — Direct fit.** Indoor, multi-view + geometry, *confirmed* windows with *confirmed* visible sun patches, plus some illumination/time variation. Usable for the task with light adaptation.
- **6–7 — Strong partial fit.** Satisfies the structural core (indoor + multi-view + geometry) and plausibly/likely contains windows and daylight, but sun-patch presence or multi-time variation is unconfirmed or absent.
- **4–5 — Component-useful.** Provides one critical missing piece well (e.g., explicit window-as-emitter modeling, or real measured sun/sky radiance) but fails on either the reconstruction side (geometry/multi-view) or the daylight-specificity side.
- **2–3 — Baseline/reference only.** Useful for reproducing IRIS itself or validating an isolated sub-module (CRF estimation, material priors, HDR calibration) but contributes essentially nothing to the daylight-through-window phenomenon.
- **0–1 — Not usable.** Wrong domain entirely (e.g., outdoor-only, object-centric, no scene structure).

## Verdict

**No existing real-world dataset contains the full required combination of indoor geometry + multi-view reconstruction + confirmed windows + confirmed cast sun patches + meaningful daylight/time-of-day variation.** This is not a close call: every real-world candidate found fails on at least two of the hard requirements simultaneously, and the failures are structural, not incidental:

- The datasets with the best reconstruction pipelines (ScanNet++, Matterport3D, FIPT real, Replica) were captured specifically to *avoid or neutralize* strong direct sunlight (consistent scan lighting, panorama-stitching exposure control, or literal removal of glass geometry in FIPT's synthetic derivation) — daylight variability is an artifact these pipelines were engineered to eliminate, not a phenomenon they were built to record.
- The datasets that do capture real, measured daylight/sun radiance well (Laval Indoor HDR, Laval PanDORA, Cali-HDR, the LEUKOS shadowband dataset) are single-viewpoint or geometry-light captures with no multi-view posed reconstruction pipeline behind them — they were built for illumination/relighting research, not for feeding a mesh+multiview inverse-rendering system like IRIS.
- The datasets that do capture genuine multi-time solar variation (NeRF-OSR, WildRelight) are outdoor-only by construction — they photograph building exteriors or open landscapes, never an interior floor lit by a window.
- The synthetic datasets that explicitly model windows as light sources (OpenRooms, I²-SDF) are useful for validating the *concept* of a window emitter module, but they are not real photographs, have no sensor noise/CRF to calibrate against, and typically render one static lighting condition rather than a moving sun.

Per the project owner's explicit instruction, this audit does not force-fit any of these to the task. **A small, targeted real-world capture is concluded to be necessary** to obtain the core phenomenon (geometry + multi-view + real windows + real cast sun patches + multiple times of day, with timestamp and geo/orientation metadata) in one coherent dataset.

## Recommended baseline datasets

For reproducing/validating IRIS itself:
- **IRIS's own 8 scenes** (ScanNet++ bathroom2/room2, FIPT real classroom/conferenceroom, FIPT synthetic bathroom/bedroom/kitchen/livingroom) remain the correct baseline reproduction target — they are exactly what the CVPR 2025 paper reports numbers on, and checkpoints are provided.
- **ScanNet++ (full corpus)** as a secondary real-geometry benchmark if the extended method needs to show it doesn't regress on IRIS's original (non-daylight) setting.

For component-level validation even though none support the full end-to-end task:
- **OpenRooms** and **I²-SDF** — for validating a window/emitter-modeling module in isolation (both provide ground-truth window geometry + emitter semantics + full HDR, letting a new "window is a directional light source" component be unit-tested against synthetic ground truth before deployment on real data).
- **Laval Indoor HDR dataset** and **Laval PanDORA HDR dataset** — for validating illumination/radiance estimation and HDR reconstruction quality against real measured indoor radiance, independent of geometry.
- **Cali-HDR dataset** and the **LEUKOS shadowband facade dataset** — for validating absolute photometric/radiometric calibration (turning LDR pixel values into physically meaningful luminance/irradiance), which is directly relevant to IRIS's existing CRF-estimation stage.
- **NeRF-OSR** — for borrowing its multi-time-of-day sun-relighting *evaluation protocol* (held-out time-of-day views, calibrated color chart, known sun geometry) even though its data is outdoor-only; it is the closest existing methodological template for how to structure held-out relighting evaluation.
- **FIPT real scenes** — for validating the HDR/exposure-bracketing and CRF pipeline that IRIS already depends on, since these are the only real scenes in IRIS's own pipeline with actual HDR ground truth.

## If targeted capture is concluded necessary: minimal capture protocol

Given the explicit guidance that "a carefully captured 2–5 scene dataset may be scientifically more valuable than thousands of uncontrolled images," the following is a deliberately small, rigorous protocol rather than a large loose one.

**Scope: 3 rooms, 3 sessions each (9 total capture sessions).**
Pick 2–3 rooms with one large, unobstructed window each (e.g., one south-facing room with a large window producing a strong, unambiguous floor sun patch at some time of day; one east- or west-facing room to get a contrasting low-sun-angle grazing case; optionally one room with a smaller/partially-obstructed window as a harder case). A 4th–5th room can be added later only if the first 2–3 prove the protocol works — do not scale up before validating the pipeline end-to-end on one room.

**Per-room fixed geometry, per-session lighting.** The room's furniture/geometry must stay physically unchanged across all sessions within that room — only the sun position changes. This is what makes the dataset scientifically useful: identical scene, varying illumination, enabling held-out relighting evaluation exactly the way NeRF-OSR does outdoors.

**Time-of-day sessions per room (3 minimum):**
- Morning (low, raking sun angle — long sun patch, likely from one side of the window)
- Solar noon or peak sun altitude for the day (shortest, most compact sun patch)
- Afternoon (raking angle from the opposite side)
Each session is a *complete* multi-view capture of the whole room, not just a photo of the patch — the geometry is shared but each session needs its own full posed image set since indirect bounce lighting, shadows, and material appearance change with sun position too.

**Multi-view trajectory per session, consistent with what IRIS/BakedSDF-style reconstruction needs.** IRIS's own README recommends camera poses be estimated with the NeRFstudio pipeline (`transforms.json`) for custom data, and geometry reconstructed with BakedSDF in SDFStudio (their customized fork). Concretely: walk a smooth, overlapping orbit/sweep trajectory around the room (60–150 images per session, phone photogrammetry style, ~70–80% frame overlap, covering the window, the sun patch on the floor/walls, and enough of the room for SDF convergence), then run COLMAP (or NeRFstudio's `ns-process-data` wrapper around COLMAP) for poses, followed by BakedSDF/SDFStudio mesh extraction exactly as documented in `README.md`'s "Customized Data" section.

**Exposure/HDR handling.** Full RAW+HDR bracketing (as FIPT did with 5-stop bracketing) is the gold standard but expensive per session at 60–150 viewpoints; a realistic minimum is: shoot RAW (DNG) at a single well-chosen exposure that keeps the sun patch from full clipping where possible (or accept some clipping and record it — IRIS itself works from LDR input, so this is not disqualifying), and additionally capture a handful (5–10) of fixed-tripod HDR-bracketed shots per session specifically of the sun patch and the window itself, for radiometric ground truth and CRF/exposure validation, following the FIPT/Laval convention.

**Metadata to preserve, all cheap to capture with modern phones:**
- EXIF timestamp on every frame (preserve, do not strip on export/resize).
- GPS coordinates from phone location services, recorded once per session (room doesn't move; confirms date/time → solar position via standard solar-position algorithms).
- Building/window compass bearing — measured with a phone compass app held flush against the window pane, recorded once per room (this, combined with GPS + timestamp, gives physically-grounded sun-direction ground truth without needing to detect the sun in-image).
- Window geometry — manually measured (tape measure) bounding dimensions and sill height, or annotated directly on the reconstructed mesh; cross-check against the mesh reconstruction.
- Optional but valuable: a LiDAR scan from an iPhone Pro-class device (Polycam, Scaniverse, or Apple's own APIs) per room, captured once (geometry is fixed across sessions), to provide an independent geometry ground truth channel alongside the photogrammetric SDF reconstruction — useful for validating/debugging the BakedSDF mesh, especially for the thin, often-problematic window frame/glass geometry.

**Why this is sufficient and why not to go bigger initially.** 3 rooms × 3 sessions with careful pose/timestamp/geo metadata already provides: (1) held-out relighting evaluation (train on 2 sessions' worth of a room's images, test on the 3rd time-of-day, analogous to NeRF-OSR's protocol), (2) physically-grounded sun-direction ground truth without manual annotation, (3) real cast sun patches at multiple angles per room, and (4) geometry good enough for IRIS's existing pipeline. Scaling to many rooms/uncontrolled images before this minimal protocol is validated risks reproducing the exact problem this audit identified in the existing datasets — large image counts with no controlled, physically-grounded daylight variation.
