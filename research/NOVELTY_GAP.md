# Novelty / Gap Analysis: Daylight-Aware Indoor Inverse Rendering

Status: derived strictly from `LITERATURE.md` (session-1 survey) and `IRIS_ARCHITECTURE_AUDIT.md` (code-level audit of IRIS on this repo). Every claim below is traceable to a specific entry in one of those two documents; where the survey did not establish something, this document says so rather than filling the gap with assumption.

Per project instructions, the word "novel" is not used anywhere below except where a specific claim is directly and explicitly supported by an absence the survey actually found.

---

## What already exists

Three separate research lines, each mature on its own terms, each stopping short of the target combination:

**1. Indoor inverse rendering (material + lighting recovery from photos of a room).** FIPT → IRIS → MAIR/MAIR++ → SIR → GLOW → AEGIR (LITERATURE.md §1–2) collectively establish a mature toolkit for recovering SVBRDF and *some* representation of incident illumination from multi-view indoor captures — LDR or HDR, single- or multi-view, with or without a calibrated light source. IRIS specifically adds LDR-only capture and joint CRF recovery on top of FIPT's factorized-lighting machinery. None of these — confirmed by direct code audit of IRIS and by abstract-level review of the others — represents illumination with any physical decomposition into direct sun + sky + transmittance; where daylight appears in their training data at all (e.g. some OpenRooms scenes have windows), it is absorbed into the same generic spatially-varying-lighting-volume or local-emitter representation used for lamps and ceiling fixtures. IRIS's own emitter-classification heuristic (saturated-pixel thresholding) actively misclassifies a sun patch as a self-glowing floor triangle rather than as evidence of external directional light — this is not a hypothetical failure mode but a directly traced code path (`extract_emitter_ldr.py`, per `IRIS_ARCHITECTURE_AUDIT.md` §4a).

**2. Outdoor daylight-aware neural rendering.** NeRF-OSR (2022) and its direct successors — SOL-NeRF, "The Sky's the Limit," SR-TensoRF, SUNDIAL (satellite domain), ROS-GS, GaRe (LITERATURE.md §3) — get closest in *representational* terms to what a daylight-aware IRIS needs: several of them (SOL-NeRF, ROS-GS, SUNDIAL) explicitly decompose illumination into a directional sun term (single spherical-Gaussian lobe or jointly-solved sun vector) and a separate sky term (low-order spherical harmonics or precomputed radiance transfer), and validate relighting against genuinely novel illumination conditions (different capture sessions/times, per the NeRF-OSR benchmark's 110-session design). This is the single closest prior work to the sun+sky *lighting model* half of the target system. Critically, however, every one of these methods is camera-outside-looking-at-a-building; none place the camera *indoors* looking *out through an aperture*, and none of them have any material/geometry concept resembling a window, glazing, or transmission — their subject surfaces are all opaque exterior facades, ground, and vegetation.

**3. Window geometry and transmissive-material recovery, as separate sub-problems.** Window aperture geometry recovery from indoor point clouds is a solved, if classical/non-learned, problem (ISPRS opening-detection energy-function method, and the semi-automated daylight-simulation reconstruction pipeline; LITERATURE.md §5) — both take the same class of input IRIS already consumes (a reconstructed indoor point cloud/mesh) and output exactly the kind of explicit aperture geometry a transmission model would need. Separately, differentiable transmissive/refractive material recovery is mature for discrete objects (differentiable refraction-tracing, TransparentGS, DiffTrans, NEMTO; LITERATURE.md §6), and Mitsuba (IRIS's own renderer) ships a physically exact thin-dielectric BSDF suited to a planar glass pane. Neither sub-problem, however, has been demonstrated integrated into a photometric material/lighting inverse-rendering optimization loop for an indoor scene — the window-geometry papers target forward daylight *simulation* (Radiance-style, with assumed glazing properties), not inverse recovery from photographs; the transmissive-material papers target isolated objects (a glass, a vase), not a wall-embedded aperture as part of a larger room-scale material/lighting fit.

**Net assessment of "how close it gets":** the field has independently solved (a) sun+sky illumination decomposition for exterior-facing daylight rendering, (b) window aperture geometry recovery from indoor 3D scans, and (c) transmissive material recovery for discrete transparent objects, and separately has a mature indoor inverse-rendering pipeline (FIPT/IRIS) that assumes none of the above. As far as this survey found, no single system combines even two of these three pieces, let alone all three inside one indoor material/lighting optimization.

## What is genuinely missing

Based specifically on what LITERATURE.md did and did not turn up:

1. **An indoor inverse-rendering system whose illumination model includes an explicit directional-sun + sky term at all.** Every indoor inverse-rendering method surveyed (§2 of LITERATURE.md) uses either a fully generic spatially-varying lighting volume/envmap or local point/area emitters; the sun/sky-explicit representations that exist (§3) are all outdoor-only. The survey found no indoor method with a SOL-NeRF/ROS-GS-style explicit sun-lobe-plus-sky-term.

2. **Any inverse-rendering system — indoor or outdoor — with a first-class window/aperture entity that transmits and refracts external illumination into an interior optimization.** The outdoor daylight papers have no interior side to transmit *into* (there is no wall, no aperture, no interior surface in their scenes at all). The indoor inverse-rendering papers have no aperture/transmission concept (confirmed absent in IRIS by direct code audit, and not claimed by any of MAIR/MAIR++/SIR/GLOW/AEGIR in the abstracts reviewed). The transmissive-material papers model transmission for isolated objects with no surrounding room and no photometric material/lighting co-optimization of the kind IRIS performs.

3. **A route from "sun patch visible on an interior floor" to a recovered sun direction.** SUNDIAL demonstrates that multi-view shadow-ray-casting can jointly solve for sun direction to high precision — but in the *outdoor* satellite domain, where the shadow-casting geometry is an object standing on open ground. The survey found no method that uses the geometrically related but distinct cue of a sun patch projected through a window aperture (the aperture acting as a pinhole/gnomon) to back out sun direction. This is a plausible adaptation of existing shadow-geometry math, not something the survey found already done.

4. **Metadata-grounded (Mode A) solar priors wired into an indoor inverse-rendering dataset pipeline.** IRIS's dataset adapters load no EXIF/timestamp/GPS metadata at all (confirmed by code audit). No indoor inverse-rendering paper surveyed loads or uses such metadata either. This absence is not really a research gap in the sense of unsolved science — solar-position-from-time+location is a closed-form astronomical calculation — but it is an absent *engineering integration* everywhere the survey looked in this specific literature.

**Stated precisely:** as far as this survey establishes, the specific combination of (explicit solar geometry) + (explicit sky illumination) + (window/aperture transmission as a first-class geometric-and-material entity) + (indoor SVBRDF/emitter decomposition of the kind FIPT/IRIS perform), fit jointly from photographs in one optimization, does not appear together in any single system found. Each pairwise or singleton piece has precedent (sun+sky: §3; window geometry: §5; transmissive materials for objects: §6; indoor material/lighting: §1–2); the four-way combination does not, within what this survey found.

## Risk of the novelty claim

Being direct about what could undercut this:

- **Field velocity.** LITERATURE.md's §2 and §3 both contain multiple 2025 and even 2026 (arXiv:2601–2606 range) entries — GLOW, AEGIR, LuxRemix, GaRe, ROS-GS, DiffTrans, TransparentGS — indicating this is an actively moving area on both the indoor-inverse-rendering and outdoor-relighting sides. A paper combining sun/sky decomposition with indoor scenes specifically could appear at any point; this survey is a September 2026 snapshot, not a guarantee of continued absence. The search terms most directly aimed at finding a pre-existing combination ("daylight aware indoor inverse rendering window sun 2025 2026 arxiv," "sun patch OR sunlight through window indoor rendering," "solar geometry indoor scene reconstruction window relighting NeRF 2025") did **not** surface a matching system — but a negative search result is evidence of absence only to the extent the search was thorough, and a targeted subagent literature pass is not equivalent to a systematic literature review.

- **Closest single candidates, flagged explicitly.**
  - **GLOW (arXiv:2511.22857, Nov 2025)** is the most recent indoor inverse-rendering paper found and explicitly addresses "global-illumination-aware" indoor recovery — but it does so via a co-located calibrated light+camera rig, deliberately sidestepping natural/daylight illumination rather than modeling it. This differentiates cleanly (opposite strategy: control the light source vs. model an uncontrolled natural one) but is worth re-checking on publication if a full paper (vs. abstract) becomes available, in case it contains an ablation or discussion of natural-light scenes.
  - **ROS-GS / GaRe (2025 outdoor GS relighting)** are the closest illumination-model matches (explicit SG-sun + SH/PRT-sky) but are confirmed exterior-only scene-representation methods (2DGS/3DGS of building facades/urban scenes) with no interior/aperture/transmission component in anything the survey retrieved.
  - **Semi-automated Indoor Geometry Reconstruction for Daylight Simulation (2025, ScienceDirect)** is the closest window-geometry-plus-daylight paper, but it is a forward-simulation pipeline (Radiance-backend, assumed glazing properties) rather than an inverse/photometric-optimization method, and was not evaluated against photographs as an inverse-rendering fitting target.
  - None of these three is a direct hit on the full combination, but each is close enough on one axis that a differentiation paragraph should name them explicitly in any paper/report this project produces, rather than letting a reviewer discover them.

- **Survey depth caveat.** This pass used web search (WebSearch/WebFetch) rather than systematic database queries (e.g., full Semantic Scholar citation-graph traversal of NeRF-OSR's ~100+ citing papers, or exhaustive arXiv cs.CV listing scans). Several abstracts (SOL-NeRF, GaRe, several 2025/2026 GS-relighting papers) could only be partially confirmed — table cells marked "not confirmed from abstract" in LITERATURE.md are genuine gaps in this survey's depth, not confirmed absences in the papers themselves. A more exhaustive pass (e.g., citation-tracing every paper that cites both NeRF-OSR and FIPT/IRIS) would strengthen or could weaken this analysis and is a reasonable next step before finalizing a novelty claim for publication.

- **The "combination is what's missing" framing is itself the main defensible claim, not any single piece.** Because every individual component (sun/sky decomposition, window geometry recovery, transmissive materials, indoor material/lighting fitting) has direct precedent, the argument for this project's contribution has to rest on the integration and the IRIS-specific engineering (the failure-mode mechanics documented in `IRIS_ARCHITECTURE_AUDIT.md` §4 — emitter misclassification, SLF absorption — are concrete and code-verified, which is a stronger and more specific claim than an abstract "nobody has done sun+window+indoor" assertion). If a close prior system is later found, the fallback differentiator is this project's grounding in a specific, audited, reproducible baseline failure mode rather than a general problem statement.
