# Reviewer challenges — hostile questions and the current honest answer

2026-09-27. Answers cite evidence in this repo. "Pending" means the experiment that
decides it has not finished. Update this file whenever an answer changes.

| # | Hostile question | Current answer | Evidence / status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Krawez et al. (RA-L 2021) already put an ephemeris sun through a window into an indoor model and predicted appearance across times of day. What is new?** | Krawez predicts appearance *forward*: reflectance is recovered separately, under lamps after sunset; the scene orientation is given; the model is diffuse radiosity; the evaluation is tracking. We would use the moving calibrated sun as the *supervision* for recovering materials, calibrate the orientation from patches, and evaluate on held-out-time images. That is a real but narrow difference. If calibration does not measurably improve material recovery over an image-estimated sun, nothing is left. | `NOVELTY_AUDIT.md` §B (full text read); T11 R1/R2 pending |
| 2 | **Li et al. (ECCV 2022) already model the window as sun + sky + ground.** | Yes. Single image. The sun direction is the brightest direction of a rendered outside panorama, then fixed. We do not claim the window sun/sky model. | `NOVELTY_AUDIT.md` §B |
| 3 | **ProjectiveShading (2026) already estimates the indoor sun direction and a sunlight map.** | Yes, from a single view, for object insertion. We do not claim sun-direction estimation; our arm B is exactly such an image-derived sun. | `NOVELTY_AUDIT.md` §B |
| 4 | **Satellite NeRFs (S-NeRF, Sat-NeRF, EO-NeRF) already use known sun directions across dates to separate shadows from albedo, and heliometric stereo uses GPS + time suns as supervision. Isn't this the same idea moved indoors?** | Largely, yes. The indoor-specific parts are window-aperture transport with interreflection and yaw calibration. Whether those parts change the outcome is exactly what T11 tests. | `NOVELTY_AUDIT.md` §C |
| 5 | **Wouldn't a larger SLF / more lighting capacity absorb the sun just as well?** | IRIS's SLF cannot make a directional sun patch: rays leaving the room return 0, so the patch must go into albedo whatever the SLF size. A generic distant envmap with ray-traced visibility *can* represent the sun. In our testbed a 128×256 envmap fits training images to 28–31 dB against 33–34 dB for the explicit sun. Whether that gap shows up in the materials is pending. | `IRIS_ARCHITECTURE_AUDIT.md` §12; EXP0030 D2; EXP0031/32 pending |
| 6 | **Is the gain just "more illumination conditions", a known principle (Eclipse, GLOW, Dynamic IR, photometric stereo)?** | The decisive comparison is C (calibrated) vs B (explicit, image-estimated) with the *same* multi-time data. Only a C > B margin is ours. | pre-registration `SOLARIR_HYPOTHESIS_TEST_ZH.md`; R1 pending |
| 7 | **In clean conditions an image-estimated sun is already accurate. Why calibrate?** | Agreed. In our clean testbed B's per-time direction error is 0.20–0.26°, and C's one-bearing calibration is off by 0.48°. Any calibration benefit must come from (a) extrapolating to unseen times and (b) conditions where the patch is hard to see: occluders, a wrong window, few views. v1 is clean; v2 adds perturbations. | EXP0030 estimates; v2 = EXP0035/36 |
| 8 | **Clouds and haze: the calibrated sun is wrong when the sun is hidden.** | Direction stays exact; intensity does not. The model learns a per-time sun intensity, which can go to ~0. Real partly-cloudy capture has not been tested. Weather data is only a soft prior (EXP0028). | EXP0028; untested on real multi-time data |
| 9 | **Noisy window geometry.** | Pending EXP0036: aperture error and an occluder. A reconstructed window will be off by centimetres. The sun patch edge moves by about (window error) × (distance / height), so C may be hurt as much as B. | pending |
| 10 | **Blinds, curtains, trees, neighbouring buildings.** | Not modelled. Anything outside the window that shadows the sun breaks both B and C. The calibrated sun then predicts a patch that is not there. We would need a per-time visibility or transmission term, which reintroduces capacity. Out of scope for v1. | not tested |
| 11 | **Specular / non-Lambertian materials.** | v1 is Lambertian only, to isolate albedo vs light. Roughness/metallic contamination by the sun is possible in IRIS but unmeasured (audit Q7). | open |
| 12 | **Your only real evidence is single-time. Where is a real room at several sunny times with held-out validation?** | There is none. Cali-HDR / Pano2Pano have one sunny time per room. The brief forbids a capture campaign in this phase. Real cross-time claims (H2, H3, H5) are **untested**, and no paper can be written without them. | `DATASET_AUDIT.md` update 2026-09-27 |
| 13 | **Within one clear day the sun path is nearly planar (Hold-Geoffroy et al. 2015). Doesn't that limit what multiple times add?** | For normals, yes, a known negative result. For albedo with known geometry the patch *moves across different texels*, which is a different kind of information. Whether 3 times help albedo is R3 / EXP0034. | pending |
| 14 | **Scale ambiguity: albedo × k with light / k.** | Unidentifiable for every arm. The primary metric is scale-aligned MAE with raw MAE reported (D0019). | D0019 |
